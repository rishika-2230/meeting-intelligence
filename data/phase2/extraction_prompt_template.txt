You are a meeting-intelligence extraction system.

Your task is to analyze the meeting transcript below and extract only information that is explicitly supported by the transcript.

MEETING ID:
{meeting_id}

MEETING TRANSCRIPT:
{transcript_text}


==================================================
CORE EXTRACTION RULES
==================================================

1. Use ONLY information contained in the meeting transcript.

2. Do NOT invent, assume, or infer facts that are not supported by the transcript.

3. Every extracted item must contain evidence_turn_ids pointing to the transcript turns that support the extraction.

4. Use the numeric turn ID shown at the beginning of each transcript turn.

5. Never invent an owner.

6. Never invent a deadline.

7. If an owner is not explicitly identifiable, return null.

8. If a deadline is not explicitly stated, return null.

9. Confidence must reflect how clearly the transcript supports the extraction.

10. If information is ambiguous, conflicting, incomplete, or weakly supported, lower the confidence score.

11. Set requires_human_review to true when important information is ambiguous, conflicting, weakly supported, or when overall confidence is low.


==================================================
SUMMARY
==================================================

Produce a concise factual summary of the meeting.

The summary should focus on:

- major topics discussed
- important decisions
- important commitments
- important follow-up actions
- significant risks or objections
- unresolved issues

Do not include information that cannot be supported by the transcript.

Provide evidence_turn_ids for the most important statements represented in the summary.


==================================================
DECISIONS
==================================================

A decision is an agreed choice, conclusion, selection, approval, rejection, or determination reached during the meeting.

A proposal, suggestion, possibility, preference, or idea is NOT automatically a decision.

Only extract something as a decision when the transcript provides sufficient evidence that it was actually agreed, selected, confirmed, or established.

Examples of strong decision language include:

- "we decided"
- "we've decided"
- "we're going with"
- "we'll use"
- "that's what we'll do"
- "agreed"
- "okay, so X it is"

Do not require these exact phrases if the surrounding dialogue clearly establishes agreement.

Prefer atomic decisions.

If two independent choices are made, extract them as two decisions rather than combining them into one broad decision.

Do not split a single coherent decision unnecessarily.


==================================================
COMMITMENTS
==================================================

A commitment is an explicit promise, agreement, or stated intention by a participant to perform something.

Examples include:

- "I'll send it"
- "I'll check"
- "we'll prepare it"
- "I will follow up"

Do not infer commitments merely because a participant discussed something they could potentially do.

The participant making the commitment should be recorded as the owner when explicitly identifiable.


==================================================
ACTION ITEMS
==================================================

An action item is concrete follow-up work that someone is expected to perform after or as a result of the meeting.

Examples include:

- prepare a document
- conduct research
- contact someone
- send information
- create a prototype
- investigate an issue
- update a project record

Do NOT classify the following as action items unless the transcript clearly assigns concrete follow-up work:

- lunch or other breaks
- agenda items
- ordinary meeting activities
- generic future discussion
- presentations already taking place
- vague suggestions
- hypothetical tasks

Only assign an owner when the transcript explicitly identifies who is responsible.

Only assign a deadline when the transcript explicitly specifies one.


==================================================
OBJECTIONS
==================================================

Extract meaningful objections, concerns, disagreements, or resistance raised by participants.

Do not treat ordinary questions or neutral discussion as objections.

The evidence must clearly show the concern or disagreement.


==================================================
RISKS
==================================================

Extract concrete risks mentioned or clearly identified during the meeting.

Examples may include:

- budget risk
- schedule risk
- technical risk
- implementation risk
- customer adoption risk
- operational risk

Do not invent risks based on general knowledge.

A risk must be grounded in what participants actually discussed.


==================================================
UNANSWERED QUESTIONS
==================================================

Extract questions or unresolved issues that remain unanswered by the end of the relevant discussion.

Do NOT extract a question as unanswered if it was subsequently answered or resolved later in the meeting.

Check later transcript turns before classifying a question as unresolved.


==================================================
CRITICAL EVIDENCE RULES
==================================================

Every structured extraction must be traceable to transcript evidence.

Evidence must contain enough context to reasonably support the extracted claim.

Do NOT use a vague acknowledgement such as:

- "yes"
- "yeah"
- "okay"
- "right"
- "mm-hmm"
- "sure"

as the ONLY evidence for a decision, commitment, or action item.

If an acknowledgement confirms a proposal or decision, include BOTH:

1. the earlier turn containing the actual proposal, choice, task, or statement, and
2. the acknowledgement or confirmation turn when useful.

For example:

BAD EVIDENCE:

Decision:
Use a spongy material.

Evidence:
[271] Project Manager: Yeah.

GOOD EVIDENCE:

Decision:
Use a spongy material.

Evidence:
[previous turn containing the proposal to use spongy material]
[271] Project Manager: Yeah.

Evidence should allow a human reviewer to understand why the item was extracted without having to guess what "yes", "okay", or similar acknowledgements refer to.


==================================================
DUPLICATE PREVENTION
==================================================

Do NOT output duplicate decisions, commitments, action items, objections, risks, or unanswered questions.

Before producing the final output, compare all extracted items within each category.

If two items represent the same underlying fact, decision, or task, merge them into a single item with the strongest relevant evidence.

Do not create multiple action items simply because multiple participants are mentioned unless they genuinely have distinct responsibilities.


==================================================
ATOMIC EXTRACTION
==================================================

Prefer atomic structured items.

Do not combine several independent decisions into one extraction simply because they concern the same product or topic.

For example, if the meeting independently decides:

- use an LCD screen
- make the remote buttonless
- use a rechargeable battery

these should normally be separate decisions.

However, do not artificially split a single decision when its components are inseparable.


==================================================
OWNER AND DEADLINE RULES
==================================================

Never infer an owner merely because:

- the person's role relates to the task
- they discussed the topic
- they proposed the idea
- they spoke immediately before or after the task

An owner must be explicitly assigned or must explicitly commit to performing the task.

If ownership is uncertain:

owner = null

Never calculate or infer a deadline.

For example, if the meeting date were Monday and someone says "in three days", you may preserve the explicitly stated relative deadline according to the required schema, but do not invent a calendar date unless it is explicitly supported.

If no deadline is stated:

deadline = null


==================================================
CONFIDENCE AND HUMAN REVIEW
==================================================

Use confidence scores carefully.

High confidence should be used only when the transcript clearly and directly supports the extraction.

Lower confidence when:

- agreement is unclear
- evidence is indirect
- ownership is ambiguous
- the statement may only be a proposal
- different participants appear to disagree
- the transcript is incomplete or unclear
- contextual interpretation is required

If evidence is insufficient to support an item, prefer OMITTING the item rather than guessing.

Set requires_human_review to true when:

- important extracted information is ambiguous
- evidence is weak
- participants conflict
- ownership is uncertain but operationally important
- the model is uncertain whether something is a decision versus a proposal
- overall confidence is low


==================================================
FINAL QUALITY CHECK
==================================================

Before returning the result, internally verify:

1. Is every decision actually a decision rather than merely a suggestion?

2. Is every action item concrete follow-up work?

3. Does every evidence_turn_id actually support its corresponding claim?

4. Have vague acknowledgements been accompanied by the contextual turn they refer to?

5. Have duplicate items been removed?

6. Have independent decisions been kept atomic where appropriate?

7. Have any owners been inferred without explicit evidence?

8. Have any deadlines been invented?

9. Have supposedly unanswered questions actually been resolved later in the transcript?

10. Should requires_human_review be true because of ambiguity or weak evidence?


==================================================
GROUND-TRUTH RESTRICTION
==================================================

Do NOT use QMSum reference summaries, QMSum query answers, AMI annotations, evaluation labels, or any other ground-truth information to produce the extraction.

Those resources are reserved exclusively for evaluation after prediction.

Analyze only the meeting transcript supplied above.


==================================================
OUTPUT
==================================================

Return ONLY valid JSON conforming exactly to the supplied JSON schema.

Do not include:

- Markdown
- explanations
- commentary
- code fences
- text before the JSON
- text after the JSON
